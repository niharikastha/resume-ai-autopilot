1. WHAT THE SPIKE CHANGED
================================================================================

Measured: 140 company slugs probed across Greenhouse / Lever / Ashby / Workable.
600 requests, 0 network errors, 34 live boards, 4,804 live postings.

  4,804   live postings across 34 boards
    699   located in India
    196   India + engineering
    115   India + engineering, minus staff/principal/manager   <- working pool
     69   ...also minus senior/lead                            <- conservative

These are LIVE postings, not daily arrivals. Postings stay open ~4-6 weeks, so
115 live roles across 22 productive boards is roughly 3 NEW relevant roles/day.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CONFIRMED - v1 was right, no change needed
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Indian companies DO use Greenhouse / Lever / Ashby. Confirmed live: Paytm,
Meesho, Groww, Navi, Zeta, Epifi, Slice, InMobi, Glance, Postman, Atlan,
Mindtickle, Druva, Netradyne, HackerRank, Sarvam, Sigmoid, ObserveAI, Turing.

The pre-spike worry that these vendors were US-only and would miss the Indian
market was wrong. v1's connector choices stand.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGE 1 - Volume expectation was off by ~2 orders of magnitude
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
v1 assumed ~800 raw postings/DAY. Reality is ~3 relevant NEW roles/day from 22
boards. The funnel shape in v1 phase 3 (800 -> 150 -> 60 -> 12) is wrong and its
thresholds must be re-derived against real data, not assumed.

CONSEQUENCE: the bottleneck of this entire project is the SIZE OF THE COMPANY
LIST, not connector code. Four connectors already cover the market. 22 productive
boards yielded ~5 reachable roles each, so the v1 output target of 8-12
applications/day needs ~80-100 productive boards, which means a seed list of
400-500 companies.

companies.yaml is therefore the load-bearing artifact of the project. In v1,
`companies:probe` was a convenience mentioned in passing. It is now a
first-class deliverable and the highest-leverage thing to build.

REVISED OUTPUT EXPECTATION: 3-6 applications/day initially, scaling toward 8-12
as the company list grows. Not 8-12 on day one.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGE 2 - Salary data for India is not 'often absent', it is absent
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  All postings with structured salary     891 / 4,804   (18.5%)
  INDIA postings with structured salary       2 / 699   ( 0.3%)

Both India instances were junk (`0-0 INR/per-year-salary`). Every real salary
found was USD on a US-located role. Lever's salaryRange and Ashby's
includeCompensation are populated - but only by US employers.

v1's three-tier design (stated -> estimated -> unknown) survives, but tier 1 is
EMPTY IN PRACTICE for Indian postings. A salary-based ranking signal has almost
no data to read.

REPLACEMENT SIGNAL: company tier. Knowing "Databricks, Bengaluru" predicts pay
far better than any posting field will. Company.tier already exists in the v1
schema; it is promoted from a salary-estimation hint to the PRIMARY pay proxy.

  STARTING BANDS - these are estimates to calibrate against real offers,
  not researched figures. Revise as data arrives.
    T1  US/global company, India engineering office      ~Rs 25-50 LPA
    T2  Well-funded Indian product startup               ~Rs 18-35 LPA
    T3  Indian mid-market product company                ~Rs 8-18 LPA
    T4  Services / staffing / consultancy                ~Rs 6-12 LPA

  Ranking prefers T1 > T2 > T3, and T4 is kept but ranked last. A STATED salary
  still overrides the tier when present. LLM salary estimation is retained but
  demoted to a tie-breaker, since it will be guessing from company name and job
  text in ~99.7% of Indian cases.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGE 3 - Drop the Indian ATS vendors. Add SmartRecruiters.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The v1 open question "should we add Keka / Zoho Recruit / Darwinbox" is now
answered: no. All three were probed.

  KEKA         Real, and robots.txt explicitly ALLOWS /careers (Disallow: /,
               Allow: /careers) - so it would be legally clean. But job data is
               client-rendered with no JSON route. /careers/api/embedjobs/all
               returns an empty 37KB shell on live tenants and FORBIDDEN ACCESS
               on others. Would need a headless browser per company.
               VERDICT: too expensive per company. Skip.

  ZOHO RECRUIT Job data is embedded in the page, but as a ~1.8MB blob of
               HTML-entity-escaped JSON. Worse: 0 of 12 real-company guesses
               were live tenants (only Zoho's own `zylker` demo responded), so
               tenant discovery is itself unsolved.
               VERDICT: skip.

  DARWINBOX    Returns a byte-identical 1,901b SPA shell for real and fake
               tenants alike. Nothing verifiable without a browser.
               VERDICT: skip.

  NOTE ON STATUS CODES: Keka, Zoho and Darwinbox all return HTTP 200 for
  non-existent tenants (Keka serves TenantNotFound.html, Zoho an error page).
  Any future connector for these MUST detect absence by body content, never by
  status code. This trap is why the first sweep over-reported hits.

  SMARTRECRUITERS - PROMOTE. Clean JSON, and it supports a native `country=in`
  filter. Bosch alone returned 543 India postings. Listed but unprioritized in
  v1; it becomes the third connector built.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CHANGE 4 - The sleeper source: US companies with Indian offices
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Not identified in v1. Highest-yield boards found, all on the four connectors
already planned, all paying above the Rs 12-18 LPA target band:

  Databricks   94 India postings (22 reachable) - best single board found
  Stripe       40      Netskope  19      Druva  17      Coinbase  15

This vein needs ZERO new connector work and is T1 on the tier scale. It should
be a deliberate seeding category, not an accident.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CAVEATS ON THE SPIKE'S OWN NUMBERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Stated so they are not mistaken for precision:
  - The slug list was guesswork (~24% hit rate), so the true universe is LARGER
    than 34 boards. The 115 figure is a floor, not a ceiling.
  - The seniority filter is title-keyword based. "SDE IV" slipped through as
    reachable; some genuinely reachable "Senior" roles were excluded. Treat 115
    as approximate. The SHAPE is reliable; the exact number is not.
  - Only 4 of the 15 v1 sources were probed. Aggregators are unmeasured.
  - One snapshot on one day. Nothing here measures seasonality.


================================================================================
2. ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================

UNCHANGED from v1 section 2. Single Nest.js 11 app + BullMQ worker, Postgres 16
with pgvector, Redis, PM2, optional Next.js dashboard later.

Pipeline stays a chain of independently retryable BullMQ jobs:

  discover -> normalize -> filter -> embed -> score -> tailor -> prefill -> record

DATA MODEL: unchanged from v1 section 3, with two additions.

  Company        + yieldStats Json   { lastFetchedAt, postingsSeen,
                                       reachableSeen, consecutiveEmptyRuns }
                 tier is now REQUIRED, not optional (see change 2).

  CompanyProbe   NEW. Records every slug guess and its outcome, so the list
                 builder never re-probes a known-dead slug.
                 { slugTried, atsType, result: 'hit'|'miss'|'tenant-not-found',
                   probedAt }

  User           NEW. role ADMIN|USER. Owns every candidate-scoped table, and
  Session        changes Application's unique index from (companyId,
                 normalizedTitle) to (userId, companyId, normalizedTitle) - the
                 one v1 invariant that roles break rather than extend.

  AppSetting     NEW. Non-secret admin-editable settings only. Secrets stay in
                 .env and are never written from the UI (2A.4).

Everything else - CandidateProfile atoms, SkillsReserve, JobPosting, MatchScore,
ResumeVariant, SourceRun - is exactly as v1 specified, plus a userId owner on
the candidate-scoped ones.


================================================================================
2A. ROLES AND ACCESS - ADMIN AND USER
================================================================================
NEW in v2. v1 assumed a single operator who was also the only candidate, so it
had no notion of accounts at all. The system now has two roles:

  ADMIN   Operates the machine AND uses it as a candidate. Sees every
          integration, every connector, every setting, the whole discovery
          funnel, and the company/probe internals.

  USER    Uses the application only. Uploads a resume, confirms the atom list,
          reviews matched jobs, approves or rejects, submits. Sees none of the
          plumbing - no ATS tokens, no connector health, no company list, no
          other user's data.

The split is by CAPABILITY, not by screen. A user is not an admin with buttons
hidden; a user's requests for admin data are refused by the server.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2A.1 THE ONE RULE THAT MATTERS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HIDING A NAV LINK IS NOT ACCESS CONTROL.

Every admin-only route carries a server-side role guard. The frontend hides
what a user cannot use, but that is ergonomics; the guard is the security. If
the two ever disagree, the guard wins and the UI is the bug.

Corollary: role is read from the server-side session on every request. It is
never read from a request body, a header, a query parameter, or anything else
the client can set.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2A.2 WHAT IS PER-USER AND WHAT IS SHARED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Getting this boundary wrong is the whole risk of adding roles, so it is
enumerated rather than left to inference.

SHARED (global, admin-managed, one copy for everyone):
  Company, CompanyProbe, JobPosting, SourceRun
    Job postings are public facts about the world. Discovering them once and
    scoring them per-user is the only sane shape - re-fetching the same
    Greenhouse board once per user would multiply outbound traffic by the user
    count for identical data, which is both wasteful and rude to the ATS.

PER-USER (owned, never visible across accounts):
  CandidateProfile      the resume, the atoms, the confirmation gate
  ProfileAtomEmbedding  derived from those atoms
  SkillsReserve         a claim about one person's real skills
  MatchScore            a score is profile-x-job, meaningless without the profile
  ResumeVariant         tailored output, contains personal history
  Application           who applied where
  ApplicationAnswers    v1's config/legal.yaml, now per-user (see 2A.4)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2A.3 THE DUPLICATE-APPLICATION INDEX CHANGES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
v1 made the anti-re-apply guarantee structural:

  Application  @@unique([companyId, normalizedTitle])

With two users that index is WRONG, and wrong in a way that looks like it works:
it would silently refuse user B's application to a role user A already applied
to. It becomes:

  Application  @@unique([userId, companyId, normalizedTitle])

The guarantee is unchanged in spirit - one person never applies to the same role
at the same company twice, still enforced by the database rather than by
application logic that could carry a bug - but it is now scoped to the person.
This is called out explicitly because it is the single place where adding roles
breaks a v1 invariant instead of merely extending it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2A.4 SECRETS DO NOT MOVE INTO THE DATABASE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Admin sees all the integration settings" does NOT mean an admin form that
writes API keys into Postgres.

  Secrets (Anthropic key, Gemini key, Telegram token, database URL, Adzuna key)
  stay in .env, readable only by the OS user running the process. The admin UI
  shows each integration's NAME, whether it is CONFIGURED, and the last time it
  was successfully used. It never shows the value and never accepts a new one.

  Reason: a web form that writes credentials to the database turns one gitignored
  file into a database column, a request body, a log line, and an HTTP response -
  four new places a key can leak - in exchange for saving one text-editor visit
  on a system with a handful of users. Wrong trade.

Editable by the admin (non-secret operational settings, DB-backed):
  company list and per-company tier / active flag, targets filters, cron time,
  notification recipients, per-user enable/disable, LLM provider preference.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2A.5 CONSEQUENCES FOR THE v1 SAFETY RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
All v1 safety rules survive; two get sharper with a second person involved.

  EEO / demographic questions. Unchanged and now more important: never
  auto-filled, never inferred, always left to "decline to self-identify". An
  admin must never be able to answer these on a user's behalf - the field is
  not admin-writable at all.

  Work authorization, notice period, current and expected CTC. v1 kept these in
  config/legal.yaml, filled once by hand and never guessed. Per-user now, and
  writable ONLY by the user who owns them. An admin can see that they are
  unfilled (it blocks the pipeline) but cannot fill them. Guessing another
  person's visa status or salary onto a real job application is worse than a
  blocked pipeline.

  Nothing here relaxes the submit boundary. There is still no submit codepath in
  any adapter, for any role. Admin is not a permission to auto-apply.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2A.6 AUTH MECHANISM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Deliberately boring, because this API is never publicly exposed (phase 0
decision: local first, then reached over an SSH tunnel).

  Password hashing   node:crypto scrypt, per-user random salt, timing-safe
                     compare. Built in - no native build, no new dependency.
  Session            opaque 256-bit random token, stored server-side in a
                     Session row, sent as an HttpOnly SameSite=Lax cookie.
                     Chosen over JWT because revocation must be immediate and a
                     stateful table gives that for free at this scale.
  Not stored         no token in localStorage - an XSS in the dashboard would
                     otherwise hand over an account.
  First admin        created by a CLI command, not by an open signup route.
                     There is no public registration; the admin creates users.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2A.7 COST NOTE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Discovery cost is flat in the user count (shared postings). Scoring and
tailoring are per-user, so LLM spend scales linearly with active users. Section
5's numbers are per-candidate from here on.


================================================================================
3. REVISED PHASE ORDER
================================================================================

v1 order was: scaffold -> LLM -> profile -> discovery -> matching -> tailoring
-> submission -> schedule.

Discovery moves to FIRST after the scaffold. Three reasons:

  1. It is the only stage with an external dependency that can fail without
     warning, and the only one whose yield is still unknown. Learn that early.
  2. It needs neither the LLM layer nor the parsed profile. Stage-1
     deterministic filtering runs off config/targets.yaml alone.
  3. It must START COLLECTING EARLY. Discovery running daily from week 1 is what
     produces the empirical atsType counts that decide which apply-adapters to
     build in phase 6 - a decision v1 correctly wanted to make from data rather
     than guesswork.

  PHASE 0    Scaffold
  PHASE 1    Discovery + company-list builder      <- was phase 2
  PHASE 2    LLM provider abstraction              <- was phase 0.5
  PHASE 3    Profile ingestion                     <- was phase 1
  PHASE 4    Matching & scoring funnel             <- was phase 3
  PHASE 5    Resume tailoring + provenance guard   <- was phase 4
  PHASE 6    Assisted submission                   <- was phase 5
  PHASE 7    Schedule, track, notify               <- was phase 6
  PHASE 8    Web UI - admin and user shells        <- NEW (see 2A)

PHASE 8 IS BEING BUILT EARLY, out of order, at the operator's request. That is a
deliberate exception with a known cost: until phases 3-6 land, the user-side
screens have nothing real to show. It is worth doing anyway because the
admin/user boundary from section 2A has to exist in the schema and the guards
BEFORE per-user data starts accumulating - retrofitting ownership onto rows that
already exist is the expensive version of this change. The read-only admin views
(funnel, connector health, companies, postings) do have real data today, from the
spike snapshot loaded by prisma/seed.ts.

From the end of phase 1 onward, discovery runs on its cron every day while the
later phases are being built. By phase 6 there is a month of real data saying
which ATS platforms actually hold the jobs.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 0 - SCAFFOLD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unchanged from v1. docker-compose (postgres:16 + CREATE EXTENSION vector,
redis:7), Nest.js 11 + Prisma + BullMQ + @nestjs/schedule, Zod-validated config
module, ecosystem.config.js, strict TS, ESLint/Prettier matching the existing
repos.

DECIDED (v1 left this open): build and run ENTIRELY LOCAL first. No server, no
SSH tunnel until after phase 5. Nothing before then benefits from being remote,
and a tunnel in the inner loop is friction for no gain. When it does deploy, the
split is v1 section 9: discovery/scoring/tailoring on the server, `submit` on
the laptop, database reached over an SSH tunnel - never exposed to the network.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 1 - DISCOVERY + COMPANY LIST BUILDER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The largest phase and the one that determines whether the rest is worth
building.

1a. CONNECTORS - build exactly four, in this order:

      Greenhouse       boards-api.greenhouse.io/v1/boards/{token}/jobs
                         ?content=true
      Lever            api.lever.co/v0/postings/{site}?mode=json
      Ashby            api.ashbyhq.com/posting-api/job-board/{board}
                         ?includeCompensation=true
      SmartRecruiters  api.smartrecruiters.com/v1/companies/{id}/postings
                         ?country=in&limit=100     (paginate on offset)

    All four verified returning 200 unauthenticated on 2026-09-05. Workable is
    deferred - it responded, but no Indian board was found on it in the sweep.

    v1's JobSource interface is unchanged:
      interface JobSource {
        readonly id: string;
        readonly kind: 'ats' | 'aggregator';
        fetch(since: Date): AsyncIterable<RawPosting>;
      }

    Each connector wraps parsing in Zod validation that LOGS AND SKIPS rather
    than throwing, and writes a SourceRun row, so a connector that silently
    starts returning zero is visible.

1b. COMPANY LIST BUILDER - the priority deliverable.

    `pnpm cli companies:probe <name|domain>` and a bulk
    `pnpm cli companies:sweep <file>`.

    The technique is proven in spike/sweep.mjs: normalize a company name to a
    slug and try it against all four ATSes. ~24% of blind guesses hit. Record
    every attempt in CompanyProbe so nothing is re-probed.

    MUST detect tenant-absence by BODY CONTENT, not status code (see change 3).

    Also fingerprint from a company's own careers page: follow the redirect
    chain, since acme.com/careers -> boards.greenhouse.io/acme reveals both the
    vendor and the token directly. More reliable than guessing when a domain is
    known.

    SEED TARGET: 400-500 companies across four deliberate categories.
      1. US/global companies with Indian engineering offices     (T1, ~150)
         Highest yield and best pay. Databricks/Stripe/Netskope/Druva-shaped.
      2. Well-funded Indian product startups                    (T2, ~150)
         Confirmed present: Paytm, Meesho, Groww, Navi, Zeta, Epifi, Slice,
         InMobi, Glance, Postman, Atlan, Mindtickle, Sarvam, HackerRank.
      3. Indian mid-market product companies                     (T3, ~100)
         The actual Rs 12-18 LPA band.
      4. Staffing / services                                     (T4, ~50)
         Kept, ranked last. Robert Half, Insight Global, Randstad, Michael
         Page, Hays, Turing.

    PRUNING: a board with consecutiveEmptyRuns above a threshold drops to a
    weekly fetch instead of daily. The list should grow without the daily run
    getting slower in proportion.

1c. NORMALIZER - as v1, with one addition.

    Strips HTML; parses stated salary ("Rs 12,00,000", "12-18 LPA", "$130k");
    infers seniority/YOE from title and text; dedupes on contentHash and
    (source, sourceJobId); cross-source fuzzy dedupe on
    (company, normalizedTitle, location) via pg_trgm.

    NEW - REMOTE ELIGIBILITY. The spike found 509 postings tagged remote but not
    India. "Remote - India" is applicable; "Remote - US" is not. The normalizer
    must resolve remoteType against eligibility rather than treating any
    "remote" string as a match, or the pool fills with jobs that cannot be
    taken.

1d. POLITENESS - as v1. Sequential per source with jittered delay, honest
    User-Agent carrying a contact address, ETag / If-Modified-Since where
    supported, SourceRun logging. The spike ran 600 requests at concurrency 8
    with 60-180ms jitter and drew zero errors or blocks; that is a reasonable
    ceiling to stay under.

    ENFORCEMENT GAP TO CLOSE IN THIS PHASE. DISCOVERY_CONTACT_EMAIL is
    .optional() in config/env.schema.ts, and correctly so - phase 0 makes no
    outbound request, and a key that is not needed yet should not stop the
    process booting. But that means nothing currently enforces the rule above.
    The check belongs in the discovery HTTP client, not in the env schema: it
    must REFUSE to issue a request when no contact address is configured, rather
    than quietly sending a UA without one. Same shape as the rest of this plan -
    fail closed at the point of use, and let the boot stay permissive.

    Until then the admin Integrations screen flags it as required-and-missing,
    which is a warning, not a guarantee.

EXIT CRITERION: discovery on a daily cron, 400+ companies seeded, and a real
measured number for relevant new postings per day. That number replaces the
invented 800 and is what phase 4's thresholds get tuned against.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 2 - LLM PROVIDER ABSTRACTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unchanged from v1 phase 0.5. One LlmProvider interface, three task definitions
(parse resume -> atoms, score a JD, tailor a resume), each defined once as a Zod
schema plus prompt.

  ClaudeProvider  @anthropic-ai/sdk. Structured output via output_config.
                  Stable prefix (atoms + rubric) marked
                  cache_control: { type: 'ephemeral' }. completeMany uses the
                  Batch API. claude-haiku-4-5 to score, claude-opus-5 to tailor.
  GeminiProvider  @google/genai. responseMimeType + responseSchema.
                  completeMany is a p-limit concurrency queue with exponential
                  backoff on 429, NOT a batch call.

Selection: LLM_PROVIDER env wins; else gemini when NODE_ENV=production, claude
otherwise. Resolved once at boot and logged.

All four v1 anti-drift defenses are retained: Zod-parse every result at the
boundary, record llmProvider+model on every MatchScore and Application row, the
cross-provider golden-set test, and a provenance guard that runs identically on
both.

Build Claude first. Gemini is not needed until deployment.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 3 - PROFILE INGESTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unchanged from v1 phase 1.

  `pnpm cli profile:ingest "Documents/astha pers/Astha Niharika - Resume.pdf"`

  1. Extract text, pass the PDF to the LLM with structured output to produce the
     atom list: { id, kind, text, tech[], metrics[], employer, dateRange }.
  2. HUMAN CONFIRMATION GATE - print the atoms, require --confirm. Everything
     downstream inherits these facts, so a bad parse must not pass silently.
  3. Embed each atom plus a whole-profile vector via local bge-small-en-v1.5
     through @huggingface/transformers (384-dim, free).
  4. config/targets.yaml: title include/exclude, seniority, locations
     (Bhubaneswar / Bengaluru / Hyderabad / Pune / remote-India),
     maxApplicationsPerDay: 15, must-have and dealbreaker keywords.

  CHANGED: salaryFloorLPA stays in the config as a soft preference, but per
  change 2 it is no longer a meaningful gate for Indian postings. Tier ranking
  does that work.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 4 - MATCHING & SCORING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Same cheap-to-expensive funnel as v1 phase 3. The COUNTS are deleted, because
they were derived from the wrong volume assumption. Every stage logs its
survivor count and thresholds are tuned against phase 1's measured data.

  STAGE 1  DETERMINISTIC (free)
           title include/exclude, dealbreakers, YOE window, location and remote
           ELIGIBILITY (not just the remote flag), postedAt freshness,
           already-applied check.

  STAGE 2  VECTOR (free, local)
           cosine distance between posting and profile embeddings, HNSW index.

  STAGE 3  LLM SCORE
           Haiku 4.5 batched / Gemini Flash. Structured output:
             { score, verdict, reasons[], missingSkills[],
               estimatedSalaryLPA, salaryConfidence }
           Profile and rubric in a cached prefix; only the JD varies.

  STAGE 4  PAY GATE - REWRITTEN per change 2.
           Rank primarily by Company.tier. A STATED salary below floor still
           rejects. An LLM estimate is a tie-breaker only, never a rejection on
           its own, because with 0.3% ground truth it cannot be calibrated.
           Unknown pay passes through - which for Indian postings is the normal
           case, not the exception.

  Because real daily volume is ~3-6 rather than ~60, stage 3 may initially score
  everything that survives stage 1 and skip stage 2 entirely. Keep the vector
  stage built - it is what allows the company list to grow 10x without the LLM
  bill following - but do not let a funnel designed for 800/day throw away
  candidates when there are only 20.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 5 - RESUME TAILORING + PROVENANCE GUARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Unchanged from v1 phase 4, which the spike did not touch and which remains the
highest-value part of the system.

The LLM receives the atom list plus the JD and returns ONLY:
  { selectedAtomIds[], rewrites: [{atomId, text}], headline, coverLetter }
It cannot emit a bullet without citing a source atom id.

provenance.guard.ts validates, and FAILS CLOSED:
  - every atomId exists in the profile
  - every number in a rewritten bullet appears in that atom's metrics
      (blocks 65% -> 85%)
  - every tech token is in the union of profile tech tags + SkillsReserve
      (blocks a silently added "Kafka")
  - employer names and date ranges byte-identical to source

Any violation -> log, discard the variant, fall back to the base resume. Guard
failure RATE is surfaced in the digest, since a rising rate means prompt drift.

Render with `docx` into the existing single-column ATS-safe layout, PDF via
headless LibreOffice. Astha_Niharika_<Company>_<Role>.pdf

Its unit tests are the first tests written (v1 verification item 1).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 6 - ASSISTED SUBMISSION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Per v1 constraint 0, this phase prepares and pre-fills. The human submits.
Adapters have NO submit codepath at all - structural, not a flag.

  interface AtsAdapter {
    readonly atsType: AtsType;
    canHandle(url: string): boolean;
    /** Fills every field it can. NEVER clicks submit. */
    prefill(page: Page, app: PreparedApplication): Promise<PrefillResult>;
  }

BUILD ORDER - now decided by phase 1's measured atsType counts, exactly as v1
section 8 wanted. Expected from the spike, subject to that data:

  TIER 1  Lever, Greenhouse. Stable field identity.
          Lever: flat names `name`, `email`, `phone`, `resume`,
                 `urls[LinkedIn]`; EEO under `eeo[...]`; custom questions under
                 `surveysResponses[{surveyUuid}][responses][fieldN]`
          Greenhouse: stable ids `first_name`, `last_name`, `email`, `phone`,
                 `resume`; custom questions `question_{id}`. It is a React SPA
                 at job-boards.greenhouse.io/{board}/jobs/{id} - drive the DOM,
                 do NOT construct a POST.

  TIER 2  Ashby, SmartRecruiters. Schema-driven, resolve fields at runtime.
          SmartRecruiters exposes .../postings/{uuid}/apply-configuration
          returning the question schema.

  TIER 3  NEW - GENERIC FORM FILLER. For custom in-house career forms, where a
          per-company adapter is never worth writing. Reads whatever fields are
          on the page, asks the LLM which profile value belongs in each, fills
          what it can, and reports a confidence per field. Less reliable than a
          real adapter, but a form arriving 70% filled is still most of the win
          given a human reviews before submitting. Turns the long tail from
          manual-link into mostly-done.

  DROPPED Workday. v1 already leaned this way; confirmed. Per-tenant accounts
          and a stateful multi-step flow for disproportionate effort. Manual
          links only.

SCREENING QUESTIONS - unchanged from v1. Three classes are NEVER auto-filled:
  - EEO / demographic -> left blank, default "decline to self-identify"
  - visa / work authorization / expected CTC / notice period -> ONLY from an
    explicit config/legal.yaml, never guessed
  - free text over N chars, or low confidence -> flagged for review

SAFETY RAILS, all default-on and all from v1: no submit codepath;
maxApplicationsPerDay cap plus per-company cooldown; the UNIQUE
(companyId, normalizedTitle) index so a duplicate fails at the database rather
than in logic; full-page screenshot and form state retained for audit; result
recorded ONLY on detected confirmation, never optimistically.

THE HANDOFF: `pnpm cli submit` opens a real non-headless Chromium with a
persistent profile and, one application at a time, navigates, fills, attaches
the tailored PDF, and pauses. The human clears the CAPTCHA and clicks submit.
No CAPTCHA solving, no bypass, no anti-detection tooling - a challenge is a
stop-and-ask, not an obstacle to route around.
